funnels are this interesting idea that a lot of what we're trying to achieve is shaped like a kitchen funnel. a couple simple examples are problem awareness funnels in sales. or lead funnels for a marketing project. in short, a customer reaches your site via youtube, then an ad, then your newsletter, then they finally buy when the right offer hits. in that case they were at the newsletter level of your funnel.

i see this at work. we have a user called TOFU. Top of Funnel User. it's a clever little acronym to describe which kinds of customers were talking about. a power user is meaningfully different than a tofu user. that's a good and useful shorthand.

i'm into that framing but think it could be a bit more expanded.

i like it because it shows how we "should" think about <span class="text-purple-600">llms</span>, AI and the new way we build products. the first part, the <span class="text-purple-600">llms</span> bit, is trying to convey that we can throw stupid random stuff at an LLM and it will maybe get you somewhere. by which i mean, it will probably actually get you somewhere. often it might even get you somewhere useful and kinda in a repeatable way. <span class="text-purple-600">llms</span> are actually pretty good at distilling a weird broad request down to a result. we can use tools and skills and all this other cruft to make them better too. but they'll still suck.

they're gonna cost a lot, lie to you about success rates a lot. only sorta satisfy customers. they're just okay. but this diagram i doodled could be thought of as a funnel. it takes you to the next thing. workflows!!

workflows are the freaking greatest. why? because they limit <span class="text-purple-600">llms</span>. you can have a workflow that's a mix between code and <span class="text-purple-600">llms</span> that jump back and forth and get you to your desired result like 99% of the time. they're easy to improve, they are easy to think about and maintain. they're cheap to run too. and they don't require cutting edge models. you can use stupid idiot models. dumb model cheap, cheap good. i like money.

now if your workflow is really freaking good and gets used all the time, it can maybe just become code. that's the dream state. code is repeatable and trustworthy. it doesn't rely on Sama or "i Da Ai doomer" to continue to exist or offer their product the way it's offered now. code lets you use regular ole ideas that we understand the ins and outs of. nice.
